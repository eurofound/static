Factsheet for measure ES-2025-23/4017 – measures in Spain

Campaign on Adverse Meteorological Phenomena, by the National Institute for Safety and Health at Work

Campaña sobre Fenómenos Meteorológicos Adversos, del Instituto Nacional de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (INSST)

Country Spain , applies nationwide
Time period Temporary, 01 June 2025 – 30 September 2025
Context Extreme Weather Events
Type Non-binding recommendations or other texts
Category Protection of workers, adaptation of workplace
– Occupational health and safety
Author Iñigo Isusi, Leyre Dilla (IKEI) and Eurofound
Measure added 20 November 2025 (updated 12 January 2026)

Background information

The National Institute for Health and Safety at Work (INSST) launched the Campaign on Adverse Meteorological Phenomena in response to the growing frequency and severity of extreme-weather events in Spain, particularly heatwaves, cold spells, storms, strong winds and sudden hydrometeorological episodes such as DANA (Depresión Aislada en Niveles Altos, or cold drops) events, which increasingly affect outdoor and mobile workers. The campaign addresses a clear preventive gap: many companies lacked structured procedures to anticipate and manage climate-related risks despite their legal duty to do so. From a regulatory point of view, the campaign is grounded in employers’ obligations under Law 31/1995 on the Prevention of Occupational Risks,. It also supports implementation of Royal Decree-Law 4/2023, which strengthened protection against extreme heat.

Content of measure

The INSST Campaign on Adverse Meteorological Phenomena applies to all companies and workers in Spain who are subject to occupational risk-prevention obligations under Law 31/1995, with particular relevance for sectors where exposure to weather hazards is intrinsic (e.g. construction, agriculture, transport and logistics, municipal cleaning and waste services, forestry, outdoor public-sector activities). Any employer required to conduct a workplace risk assessment is implicitly covered, and the campaign’s tools are designed for use by internal and external prevention services, occupational safety and health practitioners, and joint safety committees. The campaign provides technical guidance, sector-specific recommendations, evaluation templates, decision trees linked to the Meteorological State Agency (AEMET) alert levels, awareness materials, and training resources. The INSST does not allocate monetary funding, nor does it provide direct payments to companies or workers.

Use of measure

The campaign’s potential coverage is very broad, this is, all employers and workers subject to occupational risk-prevention obligations. This includes an estimated one million outdoor or partially outdoor workers across construction, agriculture, logistics, municipal services and public works. Uptake is not formally measured, as the campaign provides voluntary guidance rather than mandatory procedures, though INSST reports growing use of its materials during seasonal heat and cold campaigns. Formal data on effectiveness or the number of companies adopting contingency plans are not yet available and would need to be tracked in future updates.

Involvement

Social partners were not formally required to co-design the INSST Campaign on Adverse Meteorological Phenomena, as it is a technical initiative developed within the INSST’s statutory mandate rather than a negotiated regulation. Their involvement was therefore consultative and demand-driven, arising from repeated calls especially from Unions to strengthen prevention after several heat-related occupational fatalities. Employer organisations in highly exposed sectors, notably construction and agriculture, also conveyed operational concerns, helping INSST identify priority risks. Although they did not draft the campaign, social partners play an important role in its implementation and monitoring. Employers’ associations disseminate INSST guidance through their networks, provide training and encourage adoption of contingency plans in member companies. Trade unions use the materials to support workplace risk assessments, negotiate heat-stress protocols and report non-compliance to the Labour Inspectorate. Both sides participate in joint OSH committees, where INSST tools are used.

Views and reactions

Social partners have shown broadly positive reactions to the INSST Campaign on Adverse Meteorological Phenomena, though with differing emphases. Trade unions are highly supportive, viewing the campaign as a necessary step to address climate-related risks that had historically been underestimated. They particularly welcome the emphasis on heat-stress prevention, structured action plans and the linkage to AEMET alert levels. Employer organisations express general agreement, recognising that the campaign provides useful technical guidance and helps companies comply with increasingly demanding legal obligations related to extreme weather. Overall, employers regard the campaign as constructive and proportionate. No major organisation has expressed disagreement.

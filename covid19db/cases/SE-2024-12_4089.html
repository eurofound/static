Factsheet for measure SE-2024-12/4089 – measures in Sweden

National Strategy and Action Plan for Climate Adaptation

Nationell strategi och regeringens handlingsplan för klimatanpassning

Country Sweden , applies nationwide
Time period Temporary, 20 March 2024 – 31 December 2028
Context Extreme Weather Events
Type Other initiatives or policies
Category Promoting the economic, labour market and social recovery into a green future
– Strategic plans and programmes
Author Sydney Mc Loughlin Laewen (Oxford Research)
Measure added 08 December 2025 (updated 07 January 2026)

Background information

Sweden's revised national strategy for climate adaptation (skr. 2023/24:97) aims to develop a long-term sustainable and robust society that can cope with the effects of climate change. The strategy addresses a wide range of risks, including floods, heat waves, droughts, fires, landslides and avalanches, as well as threats to critical infrastructure such as energy and food supplies.

The strategy includes a five-year action plan (2024–2028) that specifies the work to be done. The plan is based on recommendations from the National Expert Council for Climate Adaptation and involves some 30 national authorities and all county administrative boards in its implementation.

The updated strategy builds on prior frameworks but moves “from words to action,” emphasising cross-sectoral coordination and clearer accountability among state agencies, municipalities, and private actors. It aligns with both the EU Adaptation Strategy and Sweden’s national climate targets, anchoring adaptation as an integral part of crisis preparedness and long-term sustainability planning.

Content of measure

Key elements of the strategy are to clarify the division of responsibilities between the state, regions and municipalities, and to investigate principles for financing adaptation measures.

The aim is to reduce society's vulnerabilities and systematically integrate climate adaptation into all relevant decision-making processes in order to protect citizens, businesses and ecosystems.

Sweden's national strategy for climate adaptation does not have a single, consolidated budget. Instead, funding comes from several channels, such as direct government grants to municipalities for preventive measures against natural disasters, funds from EU funds and appropriations to government agencies for their knowledge and coordination tasks. To clarify future funding responsibilities, especially for large-scale projects, a government inquiry (SOU 2025:51) has been appointed by the action plan.

Sweden’s national strategy and action plan for climate adaptation offers nationwide coverage without direct payments, with the overall goal for climate adaptation being to provide support and direction for actors in the public sector, business, and civil society, as well as for private individuals who want to make conscious choices to prevent and prepare for climate-related risks and extreme weather events. The 32 government agencies and 21 county administrative boards covered by the Climate Adaptation Ordinance are direct recipients of government grants to initiate, support and evaluate climate adaptation work in their respective areas.

Use of measure

The strategy covers the whole of society and distributes responsibility between property owners, municipalities, regional aurhorities, governmental agencies, private sector, civil soceity and citizens. Thus, the strategy and action plan targets all workers (approx. 5.3 million), citizens (approx.10.6 million), active companies (approx. 1.3 million companies), municipalities (290), and sectors (such as infrastructure, agriculture, energy- and water supply and health) vulnerable to extreme weather. No specific eligibility for financial support exists, as the strategy and action plan focus on strategic actions like responsibility- and financing models and resilience building, rather than payouts.

Climate adaptation efforts in Sweden are reviewed by a government-appointed council. The council issues a comprehensive report every five years, including recommendations for future work. The most recent report was submitted in February 2022, which is why no evaluation of the revised 2024 strategy has yet been published.

Involvement

The strategy document specifies that they have been in a process of dialogue with governmental authorities who have relevant expertise, with civil society organisations and with the Sami Parliament in Sweden. There is no recognition of the Swedish government's contact with social partners during the strategy revision process. There is no mention of official consultation processes, which may explain the lack of clarification.

Views and reactions

There are no official views or reactions to the measure from any social partners.

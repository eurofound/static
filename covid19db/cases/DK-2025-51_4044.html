Svanemøllen storm water tunnel

Background information

The Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel is a major climate adaptation project in Copenhagen and the northern part of the metropolitan area. It was initiated to overcome problems with severe flooding caused by extreme rainfall in Copenhagen. The Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel is designed to act as a massive retention basin during cloudbursts and heavy rainfall. Excess water will be stored in the tunnel until capacity is available at the treatment plant, ensuring that only treated water is discharged into the Øresund. In total, the project comprises approximately 10 kilometers of tunnel pipes, ranging between 1.8 and 5 meters in diameter, making it one of Denmark’s largest underground climate adaptation infrastructures.

Content of measure

The Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel is a joint project across Copenhagen, Gentofte, Gladsaxe, and Frederiksberg municipalities, implemented by HOFOR, Novafos, and Frederiksberg Forsyning. Eligibility is defined geographically: districts most exposed to cloudbursts and flooding, such as Nørrebro, Utterslev Mose, and Østerbro, are included. The measure targets public infrastructure, residential areas, and critical facilities. It is not a household level subsidy scheme but a collective infrastructure investment. The Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel is co financed by Novafos (42%, covering Gentofte and Gladsaxe utilities), Frederiksberg Forsyning (1%), and HOFOR, which contributes the remaining 57%. Total costs are estimated at DKK 4,160 million (EUR 554,213) in current prices, up from DKK 3,664 million (EUR 490,492) in 2022 values, with HOFOR’s share amounting to DKK 2,372 million (EUR 317,534).

Use of measure

The Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel is not a direct subsidy scheme but a collective infrastructure investment. No individual workers, citizens, or companies are eligible for payments under this measure. Financing is shared institutionally between HOFOR (57%), Novafos (42%), and Frederiksberg Forsyning (1%), covering the municipalities of Copenhagen, Gladsaxe, Gentofte, and Frederiksberg. As such, there are no realised “beneficiaries” in the sense of compensation cases; the benefit is collective flood protection for hundreds of thousands of residents and businesses in the affected districts.

Involvement

Social partners have not played a formal role in the design, implementation, or monitoring of the Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel. The measure is led by municipal authorities and public utility companies (HOFOR, Novafos, Frederiksberg Forsyning), with regulatory oversight from national environmental bodies. No involvement has been requested by institutional frameworks, nor demanded by social partner organisations. As the project concerns technical infrastructure rather than labour market or social policy, traditional social partners—such as employers’ organisations, trade unions, or EU-level social actors—have not been engaged. Their contribution is therefore not applicable in this context.

Views and reactions

Social partners have not expressed formal views or reactions to the Svanemølle Stormwater Tunnel. As the measure concerns technical climate adaptation infrastructure rather than labour market regulation or social policy, it falls outside the typical scope of social partner engagement. No documented support, disagreement, or partial endorsement has been registered by trade unions, employers’ organisations, or EU-level social actors. Their involvement has neither been requested nor initiated, and no public statements or consultations involving social partners have been identified to date.
